Amazon Ads Audit · Growth Mix

Branded efficiency can look great while category growth stays weak.

A practical audit framework for separating brand-defense spend from non-branded demand generation so blended ACOS does not hide the real growth mix.

Audit lens

Segment 1
Branded demand
Segment 2
Non-branded / category demand
Risk
Over-reliance on existing brand demand
Goal
Healthy incremental growth

Why I separate branded and non-branded

Blended account ACOS can look efficient even when a large share of paid sales comes from shoppers already searching for the brand. Branded campaigns are important for defense and control, but they do not tell the full story about market-share expansion.

BRANDEDDefense + conversion capture

Protect brand terms, manage competitor pressure and capture high-intent existing demand.

NON-BRANDEDDiscovery + category growth

Reach shoppers by problem, category, competitor and product need before they have chosen a brand.

Questions I ask during an audit

  • What percentage of spend and sales comes from branded terms?
  • How different are branded vs. non-branded CPC, CVR and ACOS?
  • Is apparently strong efficiency being carried by brand-defense traffic?
  • Which generic/category terms can support incremental market-share growth?
  • Where are competitor and product-targeting opportunities underdeveloped?
  • How does non-branded traffic contribute to organic visibility over time?

What I would optimize

I do not try to force one “ideal” branded/non-branded ratio across every business. The right mix depends on brand maturity, category competition, margins, launch stage and growth goals. The objective is visibility: understand what branded traffic is doing, what non-branded traffic is doing, and whether the combined spend matches the commercial strategy.

This is an audit methodology / strategy project. It does not claim a specific performance lift because no isolated before-and-after result is being attributed to the framework.

Why it matters

A brand can report a healthy blended ACOS while still failing to expand beyond shoppers who already know it. Separating the demand types makes it easier to protect profitability and deliberately invest in new-customer/category acquisition.
