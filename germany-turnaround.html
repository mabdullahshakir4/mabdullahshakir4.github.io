Germany · Product + PPC Growth

Improve the offer first. Then use PPC to scale stronger demand.

A Germany marketplace case study built around a simple idea: ads cannot permanently compensate for a weak product experience. Product feedback, conversion, economics and PPC need to work together.

The challenge

The product had demand potential, but weak customer feedback and product-level issues limited the value of simply pushing more paid traffic. The strategic question was not “How do we spend more?” It was “What has to improve so additional traffic can convert profitably?”

1. Start with customer complaints

The first step was to understand the reasons shoppers were dissatisfied. Product-side feedback was treated as performance data because ratings, reviews and offer quality directly affect conversion and the efficiency of every paid click.

2. Improve the product and value proposition

The project addressed the offer before leaning harder on PPC. That created better conditions for advertising to scale rather than forcing paid traffic into an experience that was still leaking conversion.

3. Support the improved offer with PPC

Once the retail side was stronger, PPC could be used more aggressively to capture demand, grow visibility and reinforce momentum. The advertising layer was managed together with margin, conversion and product economics.

4. Evaluate business outcomes, not only ACOS

The case study focused on profit and margin alongside advertising performance. In the public write-up, monthly profit moved from roughly €3K toward €9K and margin improved from roughly 23% toward 30%+, with the product becoming a top seller in the account.

The figures above reflect the case study as publicly shared. They are presented as combined business outcomes from product, offer and advertising work—not as results caused by PPC alone.

Takeaway

Good PPC can amplify a strong retail offer. It cannot permanently fix a weak one. My approach is to connect advertising with product experience, conversion, margin and customer behavior so growth is commercially sustainable.
